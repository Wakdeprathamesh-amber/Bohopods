# 14 · "FIRST LIGHT" — The Gatsby Hero Film
## Director's Treatment, Storyboard & Production Bible

> **Role:** concept planner · director · editor · colorist, in one document.
> **Method:** every one of the 27 Gatsby frames was analyzed shot-by-shot (5 parallel cinematographer passes + director's review of the anchor frames). This treatment is built on that evidence — every decision below cites the actual frame.
> **Deliverables:** (A) the 21-second looping **website hero film** — the USP; (B) a 45–60s **brand film** recut for Instagram/YouTube from the same clips.
> **Updated:** 2026-06-28 · Source frames: `public/images/gatsby/` (`ext-01..10`, `ext-exploded`, `int-01..16`)

---

## 1 · The concept

**Title:** *FIRST LIGHT — one day at the Gatsby.*

**The idea in one line:** a single day compressed into 21 seconds — you arrive through morning mist, the house breathes you inward through its green heart, the day turns golden around you, and the film comes to rest on the brand mark… then the mist closes and the day begins again. **An infinite day. An infinite loop.**

**Why this concept wins (and not alternatives):**
- *A day-cycle* gives the edit a natural arc **and** a natural loop point (mist → mist), which a hero video must have.
- It dramatizes the exact brand promise — *"inside but outside, but still inside"* — as an experience, not a claim: the camera never stops moving gently forward, always crossing thresholds (garden → portal → corridor → glass → view).
- It sells both buyers at once: the **heart** (waking inside a garden, golden evenings) and the **head** (you physically tour the layout: entry, kitchen, courtyard, bedroom, deck).
- Rejected alternatives: *pure orbit montage* (spectacle, no story), *build/assembly timelapse* (rational, not hero-emotional — saved as a beat for the long cut), *single continuous fake fly-through* (i2v can't sustain it without warping; fragile).

**Three acts:**
| Act | Beats | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| **I — ARRIVE** (0–5.5s) | mist, walkway, the sign, the dark corridor breathing open | anticipation |
| **II — BREATHE** (5.5–13.5s) | golden kitchen glide, waking to the green courtyard, the window full of clouds | immersion, awe |
| **III — GLOW** (13.5–21.5s) | the lamplit golden living room, evening life on the deck, rest on the brand mark | desire, belonging |

## 2 · The world (continuity map — read before generating anything)

The 27 frames contain **three different landscapes**. Intercutting them carelessly reads as three different properties:

| World | Frames | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Green Ghats** (misty rounded hills, cloud inversions) | ext-08, ext-10, int-01, int-03..08, int-10..16 | THE brand world. Hero film lives here. |
| **B — Konkan Coast** (sea, white dunes, golden light) | ext-03, ext-04, ext-05 | Used **only in Act III** — the palette shift is motivated by "evening golden hour", so the cut feels like time passing, not location jumping. |
| **C — Mesa/Canyon** (flat-topped cliffs, red rock) | ext-01, ext-02, ext-07, ext-09, int-02 | **Excluded from the hero loop.** Reserved for the long brand film as a "wherever your land is" montage beat. |

**Hard continuity flags (from frame analysis):**
1. **Portal color:** white travertine in ext-06/ext-07 vs **charcoal concrete in ext-10** → never cut these back-to-back. The hero loop uses only the charcoal portal (ext-10).
2. **ext-07's circular sign is garbled AI text** ("TOHOO/GATBY") — unusable except wide; the **legible** signs are in ext-10, ext-06, ext-05.
3. **Sparkle watermark glyph, bottom-right corner** on most frames (ext-01, 03, 07, 08, 09, int-07..16 confirmed) → **crop or outpaint every frame before animation** (see §6 prep).
4. int-11's mirrored wardrobe and ext-exploded's rigid parts **warp badly in image-to-video** — treated accordingly (short move / After Effects).
5. Marshall-branded speaker visible in int-02/05/13/14 — fine at web scale; flag to client for clearance comfort.

## 3 · THE HERO LOOP — shot-by-shot storyboard (21.5s, 8 shots)

Master aspect: ~2.2:1 ultra-wide (native to the renders) · text-safe: **site headline sits left-center → keep each shot's subject weighted right-of-center** · every clip generated at 5s, best 2.5–3s used.

---

**SHOT 1 — "The Sign in the Mist"** · `ext-10` · 0.0 → 3.0s
- **Frame:** charcoal board-formed portal, glass door glowing with courtyard bamboo, legible **BOHO PODS · GATSBY** disc on timber slats under a mango branch, misty green hills seen *through* two layers of glass. The strongest frame in the set.
- **Camera:** slow dolly-in on the portal axis (walking pace, ~4%/s scale change), dead level.
- **Motion to prompt:** mist drifting between hill layers behind both glass planes; bamboo stirring behind the door glass; mango-leaf shadow dapple on the slats; faint pendant sway inside.
- **Role:** poster frame (frame 0 = the hero's `poster` image), brand mark visible in the first second — the film "signs" itself immediately.
- **Cut out:** as the portal frame edges begin to leave frame → **CUT on forward motion** (the next shot continues the same push).

**SHOT 2 — "The Breath"** · `int-01` · 3.0 → 5.5s
- **Frame:** dark one-point entry corridor compressing toward glowing glass; behind it the planted lightwell (fiddle-leaf, bamboo, ferns), clerestory of hazy ridgelines above.
- **Camera:** continue the dolly-in down the corridor — **the same vector as Shot 1** (invisible-cut logic: door → corridor is how you'd walk it).
- **Motion:** foliage sway behind glass; mist on the far ridges; palm shadow creeping on the floor; light bloom through the clerestory. Prompt "static glass, moving plants" (analyst note — protects reflections).
- **Why here:** the darkest frame of the set placed early = the film's *inhale*; everything after feels brighter because of it (contrast pacing).
- **Cut out:** as the glass panel nears full-frame, **light-wipe** — the bloom of the lightwell whites the exposure up 10% → next shot.

**SHOT 3 — "Golden Glide"** · `int-04` · 5.5 → 8.0s
- **Frame:** golden-hour kitchen/dining — terrazzo island, cane lounge chair, curved corner glazing packed with bird-of-paradise, gold misty valley beyond.
- **Camera:** **lateral dolly right-to-left with the timber-slat wall strobing past in near-foreground** — the analysis calls this the best parallax move available in the set; it's the film's one lateral move, so it reads as "gliding through the home".
- **Motion:** valley mist rolling; tropical leaves nodding; dust-in-light shimmer.
- **Text sync:** site headline ("Own the view.") sits over the calm left third as slats exit.
- **Cut out:** settle 6 frames before the cut (micro-rest), then cut on stillness.

**SHOT 4 — "Waking in a Garden"** · `int-16` · 8.0 → 10.5s
- **Frame:** from the foot of the bed — rumpled linen in FG, the glass-boxed bamboo courtyard as a green wall, corridor running through the pod to deck + hazy hills. The purest *inside-but-outside* statement in the set (8.5/10).
- **Camera:** slow dolly-in from the bed toward the courtyard glass (a waking POV rising into the green heart). **Avoid rightward drift** — the mesh pendants at right-edge will warp (analyst caution).
- **Motion:** bamboo canes + monstera trembling inside the courtyard; dappled light shimmering down the corridor; haze on the distant hills; duvet edge stirring faintly.
- **Cut out:** cut as courtyard glass reaches ~70% of frame width.

**SHOT 5 — "The Window Full of Clouds"** · `int-14` · 10.5 → 13.5s
- **Frame:** the symmetric bedroom: square window dead-center over the bed, and through it a **cloud inversion pooling between mountain ridges** at golden dawn; lit pendant orbs; walnut slat ceiling.
- **Camera:** classic center-axis dolly-in over the bed toward the window until the view dominates — "the room frames the view". Symmetry keeps AI warping low (analyst note). The push naturally exits the ceiling AC from frame — intentional.
- **Motion:** THE money motion — clouds sliding and pooling between ridges (the most believable i2v effect there is); pendant glow breathing; courtyard foliage at frame-left.
- **This is the awe beat.** Longest-feeling shot; let it be still except the sky.
- **Cut out:** **match-cut on the window** → Shot 6's glass wall occupies the same zone of frame (window → window, view → view).

**SHOT 6 — "Golden Hour Indoors"** · `int-05` · 13.5 → 16.5s
- **Frame:** the interior money frame (9/10): curved boucle sofa, glowing orb lamp, island, and beyond the glass a plunge pool and a **misty gorge with a waterfall**, sky peach-to-lavender, practicals lit.
- **Camera:** very slow dolly-in toward the central glass with mild parallax off the sofa edge — hold the symmetry, "let the fog do the acting" (analyst).
- **Motion:** fog thinning/drifting across ridges; waterfall movement; lamp glow breathing; light level dropping almost imperceptibly (day → evening inside one shot).
- **Grade note:** this frame *is* the brand board — the whole film's grade is matched to it (see §5).

**SHOT 7 — "Evening Life"** · `ext-03` · 16.5 → 19.0s
- **Frame:** golden-hour coastal deck — floating roof, outdoor sofas, the pool table under the canopy, backlit grass plumes, white dunes and open sea (9/10).
- **Camera:** **rising crane-up from grass level** over the deck to reveal the ocean — the film's only vertical move, saved for the emotional lift.
- **Motion:** waves rolling on the shoreline; backlit grass swaying; palm shadows shifting on the deck; soffit lights already glowing.
- **Why the location change works:** the palette has been walking toward gold since Shot 3; the sea reads as "the day went on", not "different house". (World B is only ever entered here, in Act III.)
- **Cut out:** cut at the top of the crane as the sea horizon settles level.

**SHOT 8 — "Come Stay"** · `ext-05` · 19.0 → 21.5s → loop
- **Frame:** the arrival walkway through a meadow of plume grasses and birds-of-paradise to the entry portal, **legible circular brand sign** on the slats, sea haze, early golden light.
- **Camera:** walking-pace dolly-in that **eases to rest** with the sign readable — the film literally walks you to the door and stops. (Keep the move gentle so the sign text doesn't warp — analyst caution.)
- **Motion:** the meadow **en masse** (the set's biggest motion asset); palm fronds overhead; clouds over the sea.
- **Loop engineering:** in the final 12 frames, drift a soft white **mist bloom** in from the edges (10→25% opacity) and dissolve through it back to Shot 1's misty portal. Mist-out → mist-in = **invisible loop seam**. Shot 8's warm gold hands to Shot 1's cool haze under the mist so the cycle restarts at "morning".

**Pacing curve:** 3.0 · 2.5 · 2.5 · 2.5 · 3.0 · 3.0 · 2.5 · 2.5 — long-short-short rhythm with the two 3s "awe holds" at Shots 5–6, i.e. tension → release exactly at the middle. No shot under 2.5s (premium = unhurried); nothing over 3s (it's a hero, not a film festival).

## 4 · Per-shot generation prompts (image-to-video)

**Tool routing** (use what you have in Higgsfield; equivalents noted):
- **Mist/cloud shots (1, 2, 5, 6):** Kling 2.x Pro or Veo — best atmospheric particle motion. Higgsfield preset: *Dolly In*, motion strength LOW (3/10).
- **Parallax/lateral (3) & crane (7):** Runway Gen-4 (Camera Control: horizontal / crane) or Higgsfield *Lateral Slide* / *Crane Up* presets.
- **Meadow + settle (8):** Kling (grass simulation is its strength), motion strength 4/10, camera ease-out.

**Universal prompt suffix (append to all):**
`static architecture, glass and window frames rigid and undistorted, no people, no added objects, photorealistic, luxury architectural film, gentle motion only, locked exposure, no camera shake, cinematic, 24fps`

**Universal negative prompt:**
`warping, morphing walls, melting glass, extra furniture, people, text artifacts, flicker, oversaturation, camera shake, fast motion, zoom burst`

**Generation protocol per shot:** 3 takes minimum → pick for (a) architecture rigidity, (b) motion believability, (c) a clean 2.5–3s segment with an ease. Judge glass lines and the sign first — one warped mullion kills the premium feel. Expect Shots 3 and 7 to need the most takes (biggest camera moves).

## 5 · Edit assembly, grade & sound

**Assembly (DaVinci Resolve / Premiere / CapCut):**
1. Lay shots on the pacing curve above; every cut lands on motion (the eye never notices).
2. **Speed:** run every clip at 85–95% with optical-flow retiming — slightly slower than life = luxury.
3. **Transitions:** hard cuts only, except the Shot 2 light-wipe (+10% exposure bloom, 6 frames) and the Shot 8→1 mist dissolve (18 frames).
4. **Micro-rests:** freeze the last 4–6 frames of Shots 3 and 8 by 50% (ease-to-still) — the "settle" that makes motion feel intentional.

**Grade (one LUT for all 8 clips — match target is int-05):**
- Warm the highlights (+8 toward amber), drift shadows toward **deep sage/forest** (the site's `#36441f` family), lift blacks +3 (soft filmic floor, never crushed).
- Desaturate global −8% *except* greens (foliage stays alive) and the warm practicals.
- **Film grain 4%**, fine — matches the site's `.grain` overlay so video and UI feel like one material.
- Shots 1–2 stay coolest (morning), 3–6 warm progressively, 7–8 peak gold: the grade *is* the clock.

**Sound design (brand-film cut only — the web hero is muted by law of autoplay):**
- Bed: valley birdsong + soft breeze (Acts I–II) → evening crickets + distant surf (Act III).
- Accents: one soft wood-on-stone footstep at Shot 1's cut; a barely-there whoosh under the Shot 7 crane; felt-piano note (single, warm) at the Shot 5 cloud reveal; a low warm swell resolving as Shot 8 settles.
- No VO. If music: sparse felt piano + tanpura drone at −20 LUFS, nothing "corporate".

## 6 · Pre-production checklist (do BEFORE generating)

1. **Clean every source frame:** crop or content-fill the **sparkle watermark** (bottom-right) on all frames used. Never feed it to the video model — it will smear across frames.
2. **Pre-crop each still to 16:9** (models output 16:9; the renders are ~2.2–2.5:1). Per-shot crop guidance: keep the *right-weighted* composition (site text lives left). For Shot 5 keep the window dead-center; for Shot 1 keep sign + portal both in frame; for Shot 8 keep sign right-of-center.
3. ext-10 / ext-05 sign check at 200% zoom after generation — regenerate on any warp.
4. Lock seeds/settings per shot; note them (re-generation reproducibility).
5. Do **not** i2v the exploded diagram (rigid-body warping) — that beat, if used in the brand film, is an After Effects position-animation of the actual layers, or a simple cross-dissolve stack.
